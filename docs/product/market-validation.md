# Market Validation Plan

Status: hypotheses to test, not completed market proof

## 1. Core hypothesis

Creators who already produce long-form conversational or educational video experience enough pain finding and editing short clips that they will repeatedly use and eventually pay for a tool that produces credible first drafts.

## 2. Why validation is still required

Automatic clipping is an established software category, so technical possibility is not the question. ClipMine must prove a narrower advantage:

- Better standalone clip coherence
- Less correction and reframing work
- Clearer rights/privacy controls
- Reliable phone-first review
- A workflow matched to a specific creator segment

“AI clips videos” is not differentiated on its own.

## 3. Competitive categories

Research should compare ClipMine against:

- Automatic long-to-short clip generators
- Podcast recording suites with clip features
- General video editors with AI tools
- Caption-first mobile editors
- Human freelance editors and content agencies
- Manual editing in platform-native tools

Maintain a dated evidence table during discovery covering target user, input limits, editing depth, output quality, pricing, publishing integrations, privacy and common complaints. Do not treat marketing claims as verified capability.

## 4. Candidate beachhead segments

### A. Interview/podcast creators

Advantages: frequent long recordings, obvious multi-clip potential, repeat workflow.  
Risks: crowded tooling and complex multi-speaker framing.

### B. Educators and expert creators

Advantages: dense useful content; standalone explanations can be scored clearly.  
Risks: lower publishing frequency and topic-specific context.

### C. Founder/business content teams

Advantages: value time savings and brand consistency.  
Risks: approvals, brand controls and team workflows may expand scope.

### D. Streamers/entertainment creators

Advantages: high content volume.  
Risks: quality relies more on visual/game context, humour and fast-changing formats.

Recommended validation order: interview/podcast creators and educators first, then select one based on observed frequency, current cost and willingness to trial.

## 5. Discovery questions

Ask about past behaviour rather than whether the idea sounds good:

- Walk me through the last long video you turned into clips.
- Who found the moments and how long did it take?
- What made a chosen moment usable or unusable?
- Which step caused the most rework?
- What tools or people did you use and what did they cost?
- How many long videos and clips do you publish in a typical month?
- What happens when you do not make the clips?
- Which content must never be uploaded to a third-party service?
- Show me an automatically generated clip you rejected and explain why.
- What result would make you trust this on the next recording?

Do not lead with feature descriptions until the current workflow is understood.

## 6. Validation experiments

### Concierge test

Manually run the proposed pipeline behind the scenes for permissioned videos and return five candidates. Measure which candidates users select, corrections and turnaround expectations.

Pass signal: users return with another video without being chased and at least half export/use one candidate.

### Candidate-only prototype

Return timestamped moments and reasons before building a full editor.

Pass signal: top candidates beat the creator's own quick scan or a simple transcript baseline.

### Render prototype

Produce one vertical captioned clip from an accepted range.

Pass signal: most corrections are light text/boundary adjustments rather than full recreation.

### Price/allowance test

Show users realistic monthly source-minute allowances informed by actual cost. Ask them to choose or decline a plan in a credible beta signup—not only state hypothetical willingness.

## 7. Evidence thresholds before full MVP

- At least 10–15 interviews in one segment
- At least 20 permissioned source videos evaluated
- Repeated use from at least five creators
- Measured top-candidate usability above defined baselines
- Known median edit burden and processing cost
- Clear reason users choose ClipMine over their current workflow

These are planning gates, not claims that validation has already happened.

## 8. Risks to disprove early

- Users want a full editor rather than candidate generation
- Source upload is too inconvenient compared with URL import
- “Best moment” varies too much by creator for a general model
- Rendering cost makes acceptable allowances unattractive
- Users will not trust private/unpublished footage to the service
- Existing bundled tools are good enough at no extra cost
- The creator's bottleneck is posting strategy, not editing

## 9. Research repository

Store dated, consented and anonymised summaries outside public source control if they contain participant information. Commit only aggregated findings, changed hypotheses and product decisions.
