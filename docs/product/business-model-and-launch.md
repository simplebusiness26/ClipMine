# Business Model and Launch Strategy

Status: hypotheses pending cost and customer validation

## 1. Positioning

ClipMine is an AI first-pass short-form editor for creators with valuable long-form footage.

Position around:

- “Find complete moments, not random fragments.”
- Lower editing time and correction burden
- Creator control and private-by-default processing
- Platform-independent exports

Avoid promises of guaranteed virality, views or income.

## 2. Business model

Recommended model: subscription tiers with included source-video minutes and sensible processing limits. Video processing has real variable cost, so unlimited plans are risky before measured usage exists.

Potential structure after validation:

| Plan | Intended user | Possible shape |
|---|---|---|
| Trial | New user testing one real project | Small one-time minute allowance, watermark decision tested |
| Creator | Regular solo creator | Monthly source minutes, core templates and exports |
| Pro | High-frequency creator/small operator | More minutes, brand kits, faster queue and later publishing |
| Team | Agencies/content teams | Seats, approvals, shared brands and higher limits |

Do not set final prices until transcription, model, render, storage, support and payment costs are benchmarked. Allow users to buy additional capacity only with clear price and spend controls.

## 3. Unit economics to measure

Per source minute and per exported clip:

- Upload/storage and retention
- Proxy/audio compute
- Transcription
- Language/embedding analysis
- Visual analysis
- Preview/final render compute
- Download egress
- Provider retry/waste
- Support and payment cost allocation

Track gross margin by plan without exposing private content in billing data.

## 4. Launch sequence

### Stage 1 — Design partners

- Recruit 5–10 creators in the chosen segment.
- Process their real permissioned footage through the technical proof.
- Observe selection/editing rather than relying on surveys.
- Provide direct support and record recurring failures.

### Stage 2 — Private alpha

- Invitation only
- Strict upload/concurrency limits
- Export only
- One supported language/source profile initially
- Weekly product-quality review

### Stage 3 — Paid or commitment beta

- Add a transparent allowance model
- Validate repeat use and price sensitivity
- Publish privacy/rights/support materials
- Expand templates and formats only from evidence

### Stage 4 — Distribution integrations

- Apply for platform permissions independently
- Add authorised upload/draft flows one platform at a time
- Keep downloads available as a reliable fallback

## 5. Acquisition channels to test

- Direct outreach to small podcast/education creators with a specific sample offer
- Partnerships with podcast editors or production studios
- Creator communities where self-promotion is permitted
- Educational content showing how to identify self-contained moments
- Referral from design partners
- Before/after workflow case studies with explicit permission

Do not mass-spam creators or reuse their footage in marketing without separate consent.

## 6. Activation and retention

Activation event: user uploads a real video and exports/approves at least one clip.  
Retention signal: user returns with another source video within their normal publishing cycle.

Improve activation by:

- Supporting one reliable source profile first
- Showing honest progress and recovery
- Returning a small number of strong candidates
- Making first corrections fast on a phone
- Explaining privacy and deletion before upload

## 7. Launch metrics

- Signup-to-valid-upload rate
- Valid-upload-to-candidate-ready rate
- Candidate-ready-to-export rate
- Median accepted clips per project
- Median edit time/corrections per export
- Repeat project rate by creator cycle
- Cost per processed minute and per export
- Support contacts and failures per project
- Trial-to-paid/committed conversion when applicable

Views on social platforms may inform later quality learning, but they are affected by account, topic, timing and distribution. Do not use them as the only measure of ClipMine quality.

## 8. Launch blockers

- Brand/trademark clearance incomplete
- No measured processing cost or hard spend caps
- No verified end-to-end deletion
- Unclear rights/takedown route
- Cross-tenant or private-media access risk
- Candidate quality not better than simple baselines
- Core phone flow unusable on weak networks
- No operational response for stalled jobs/provider outage

## 9. Brand checklist

Before public naming/marketing investment:

- Search relevant UK and intended-market trademarks
- Check domain and major social handles
- Search app stores and software categories
- Review pronunciation, spelling and international conflicts
- Decide whether ClipMine remains the final name
